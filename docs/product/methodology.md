# Methodology

The aio flow comes from the principles that education research identifies as the ones that work best. This document lists those principles and shows how they become the stages of a session. The central thesis: a one-on-one tutor diagnoses you and readjusts the teaching after each answer.

## The evidence-based principles

Research converges on these principles:

- **Diagnose prior knowledge.** Ausubel: "the most important single factor influencing learning is what the learner already knows".
- **Define the goal first, and design the assessment from it.** This is the _backward design_ of Wiggins and McTighe.
- **Explicit instruction in small steps, with a check at each step.** This comes from Rosenshine's principles and from cognitive load theory.
- **Worked examples, with the help removed a little at a time.** This is the "I do, we do, you do" sequence.
- **Retrieval practice.** Pulling from memory teaches more than rereading.
- **Spacing.** You review on different days, at growing intervals.
- **Interleaving.** You mix problem types instead of solving blocks of the same type.
- **Fast, specific feedback.**

## The flow that follows from them

The principles do not form one official sequence, but they point to this order:

```text
probe → plan → teach (with a check at each step) → guided practice → independent practice
                                                                          ↓
                                        spaced review (days/weeks), interleaved
```

The LearnHarness flow, `probe -> plan -> teach -> practice -> recall`, gets two points wrong, and this flow fixes both.

## Recall happens everywhere

Recall is not the last stage. It appears at the start of each lesson, as a review of what came before. It also appears inside teach, as the check on each step, and later, spaced over days and weeks. Practice and recall are almost the same thing, because practice already pulls from memory.

## What makes learning stick happens after the session

The stretch from probe to practice fits in one session. What makes the learning stick is what comes after: spaced reviews, at intervals that keep growing. For that reason aio must schedule the review of each item, practice problems included, and not only isolated facts.
